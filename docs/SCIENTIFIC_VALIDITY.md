# Scientific validity

The checked-in target is a retrospective record-linkage proxy. Positive labels represent accepted associations with cataloged recalls. Rows without accepted links can reflect missing listings, insufficient identifiers, failure to generate a candidate, or an actual unrelated incident. Linkage precision and recall require an independent, manually adjudicated reference set.

The original application's random stratified 80/20 row split permits repeated products and manufacturers across folds. It excludes direct linkage columns via a feature allowlist, but manufacturer, brand, model and text overlap the evidence used to construct labels. Published or edited incident text can mention a recall. Neither report dates nor publication dates establish that the text and field values are the versions available at score time. The older notebook's chronological experiment lacks a fixed follow-up horizon and as-of feature verification.

The rebuilt evaluation compares stratified random rows, product-proxy groups, and chronological partitions. Chronological boundaries use distinct report dates and purge product proxies from earlier partitions when they recur later. Group keys derived from identifiers and normalized descriptions cannot reliably resolve aliases or all duplicate products. Manufacturer overlap remains possible by design. All trained transformations fit on training only. Validation controls hyperparameters, model selection and score thresholds. A separate calibration partition supports sigmoid calibration to the dataset's proxy label.

A chronological holdout on retrospective labels measures robustness to report-date shift. Prospective validation would require timestamped original report snapshots, a frozen recall catalog as of evaluation, a predefined horizon, right-censoring rules, late-publication handling and label maturity at training cutoffs. The rebuilt linkage mode implements a date-gated association proxy and deliberately does not turn unresolved matches into negatives. Neither artifact estimates an actual regulatory risk level or establishes advance warning.

## Measured leakage indicators

Of 756 random-test observations, 105 shared a product proxy and 580 shared an exact nonblank manufacturer string with an earlier partition. Grouped evaluation reduced product-proxy overlap to zero while retaining manufacturer overlap for 565 of 755 test rows. Chronological purging excluded 269 rows and left zero overlapping product proxies and 448 overlapping manufacturer strings among 756 test rows. These counts depend on imperfect proxy keys. The random test uses the historical 20% seed-42 row-split protocol with new internal partitions and a different feature pipeline.

The tracked CSV lacks recall announcement dates. Its publication dates lag report dates by a median of 25 days. The word recall or an inflection appears in 84 of 420 linked incident narratives and 512 of 3,360 other narratives. This flags possible post-event evidence without proving the timing of any specific text. In the separately located 3,271-row earlier artifact, 42 of 416 positive associations have equal incident and announcement dates and 374 have later announcements. Its optional within-12-month flag is true for 249 positives. Do not transfer these timing counts to the tracked dataset.

The group-purged chronological result (AP 0.144 against prevalence 0.128, F1 0.019) demonstrates a substantial loss of model utility under this evaluation setting. Time shift, entity purging, different class distributions, and incomplete linkage can all contribute. The comparison cannot isolate their causal effects.


## Original export provenance

All 3,780 tracked report IDs occur in the supplied incident export. Each of the 416 earlier audit pairs has a report ID and recall number in the supplied exports, and the audit's recall index points to that exact raw recall number in all 416 cases. This provides evidence that raw recall row order can recover historical references. Of the 241 positive report IDs shared by the tracked and earlier data, 238 retain the same recall index. The other three may reflect revised decisions.

Applying the raw row-order mapping to the tracked positives gives 44 same-day, 192 one-to-365-day, and 184 later-than-365-day matches. These inferred dates clarify why the supplied source files cannot turn the original labels into a fixed-horizon target by themselves. The earlier audit contains just 207 date-eligible pairs out of 416 under the current one-to-365-day definition. Its labels were generated by the previous linkage methodology and do not constitute human-confirmed ground truth.

Publication timing further narrows apparent lead time. Of the 192 inferred matches within one year after report filing, 39 had already been announced by the public report date. Across the 420 historical positives, 83 inferred announcements occurred on or before publication. A system that consumes public reports would need to anchor evaluation to public availability and measure outcomes from that date.
